`identity/username/conflicts.yml` holds the rules that settle two accounts wanting one username, and `identity/username/messages.yml` what players see when it happens. [Usernames and conflicts](../../../admins/guides/accounts/usernames/index.mdx) explains when a conflict comes up and what the default rules do.

## conflicts.yml

<Tabs items={["YAML", "JSON"]}>
  <Tab value="YAML">
```yaml
outcomes:
  rename-holder:
    pattern: "{username}_{provider}"
    tooLong: SHORTEN
    taken: NUMBER
    holderOnline: KEEP_PLAYING
  rename-arriving:
    pattern: "{username}_{provider}"
    tooLong: SHORTEN
    taken: NUMBER
  refuse: {}
providers: {}
rules:
  - arriving: { guarantees: [OWNED_USERNAME] }
    holder: { guarantees: [] }
    trigger: FIRST_JOIN
    outcome: rename-holder
  - arriving: { guarantees: [OWNED_USERNAME] }
    holder: { guarantees: [] }
    trigger: PROVIDER_RENAME
    outcome: rename-arriving
  - arriving: { guarantees: [] }
    outcome: refuse
  - outcome: refuse
```
  </Tab>
  <Tab value="JSON">
```json

    "refuse": {}

    { "arriving": { "guarantees": [] }, "outcome": "refuse" },
    { "outcome": "refuse" }
```

<Setting
  name="rules"
  takes="a list of rules"
  affects="which outcome settles a conflict"
>

A rule without conditions is the least specific one and settles everything no other rule does.

</Setting>

#### Which rule applies

Of the rules that apply to a conflict, the most specific one wins:

1. a rule naming provider ids on more sides beats one naming fewer;
2. then a rule naming guarantees on more sides beats one naming fewer;
3. then a rule that also names a `trigger` or `arrivedVia` beats one that does not.

Among equally specific rules, the first one in the list wins. When two equally specific rules can apply to the same conflict but settle it differently, the console warns on start and on reload, because the second one never applies there. When no rule applies, the arriving player is refused.

#### rules[].arriving and rules[].holder

What the rule asks of the joining player's provider and of the holder's provider. Leave a side out to accept any.

<Setting
  name="guarantees"
  depth={5}
  takes="a list of guarantees, such as `[OWNED_USERNAME]`"
  affects="which providers the side may stand on"
>

The provider must have exactly these guarantees: `[]` asks for a provider that guarantees nothing, such as Credential. Leave the field out to accept any provider. `OWNED_USERNAME` is the guarantee of providers that own their players' names, such as Premium.

</Setting>

<Setting
  name="providers"
  depth={5}
  takes="a list of provider ids, such as `[premium]`, in any case"
  affects="which providers the side may stand on"
>

</Setting>

<Setting
  name="rules[].trigger"
  depth={4}
  takes="`FIRST_JOIN` or `PROVIDER_RENAME`"
  affects="which situation the rule applies to"
>

Leave it out for both. See [When a conflict comes up](../../../admins/guides/accounts/usernames/index.mdx#when-a-conflict-comes-up).

</Setting>

<Setting
  name="rules[].arrivedVia"
  depth={4}
  takes="why the joining player's provider was picked, such as `HOSTNAME`"
  affects="whether the rule applies only to players who reached their provider that way"
>

`HOSTNAME` means the player joined through one of the provider's `entrypoints`. Leave it out for any reason.

</Setting>

<Setting
  name="rules[].outcome"
  depth={4}
  takes="an outcome id, such as `rename-holder`, or `{ id: ..., with: { ... } }` to change some of its settings for this rule only"
  affects="how the conflict is settled"
>

| Outcome | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| `refuse` | The joining player is refused with `pipeline.policy.taken`; the holder keeps the name. |
| `rename-arriving` | The holder keeps the name; the joining player's account gets a renamed one. |
| `rename-holder` | The joining player's account gets the name; the holder gets a renamed one. |

Addons and providers may add outcomes of their own; their ids appear under `outcomes`. A rule naming an outcome that does not exist is reported as an error in the console on start and on reload, and refuses the joining player instead.

```yaml
outcome:
  id: rename-holder
  with:
    holderOnline: KICK_HOLDER
```

</Setting>

### outcomes and providers

An outcome's settings come from three places, each stating only what it changes:

1. the rule's `with`, which wins;
2. `providers.<provider>.<outcome>`, for the provider of the account the outcome renames: the holder's for `rename-holder`, the joining player's for the others;
3. `outcomes.<outcome>`, which Identica writes with the outcome's defaults.

```yaml
providers:
  credential:
    rename-holder:
      pattern: "{username}_offline"
```

<Setting
  name="pattern"
  depth={4}
  takes="a string"
  default='`"{username}_{provider}"`'
  affects="the renamed username of `rename-holder` and `rename-arriving`"
>

`{username}` is the username being renamed and `{provider}` the id of the renamed account's provider. Characters Minecraft does not allow in a username, anything but letters, digits and `_`, are left out.

</Setting>

<Setting
  name="tooLong"
  depth={4}
  takes="`SHORTEN` or `REFUSE`"
  default="`SHORTEN`"
  affects="what happens when the renamed username is longer than Minecraft's 16 characters"
>

`SHORTEN` shortens the original username until the result fits: `Christopherson` becomes `Chris_credential`. `REFUSE` refuses the joining player instead, and the holder keeps the name.

</Setting>

<Setting
  name="taken"
  depth={4}
  takes="`NUMBER` or `REFUSE`"
  default="`NUMBER`"
  affects="what happens when another account holds the renamed username"
>

`NUMBER` adds a number from 2 to 9, such as `Steve_credential2`, and refuses the joining player when all of them are held. `REFUSE` refuses at once.

</Setting>

<Setting
  name="holderOnline"
  depth={4}
  takes="`KEEP_PLAYING` or `KICK_HOLDER`"
  default="`KEEP_PLAYING`"
  affects="what `rename-holder` does with a holder who is online"
>

With `KEEP_PLAYING`, the holder keeps playing under the name until leaving and plays under the renamed name from the next join on. Both stored names change at once, so the joining player plays this session under a name made with the same `rename-holder` settings, such as `Steve_premium`, and under the name itself from their next join on, once the holder has left.

With `KICK_HOLDER`, the holder is disconnected with `pipeline.policy.holderReplaced`, so the name is free from then on. The proxy names the joining player while the holder is still connected, so the joining player also plays this first session under a name made with these settings.

</Setting>

#### Names made without a rule

Identica uses the `rename-holder` settings in two cases no rule decides:

- Two accounts of the same provider with `OWNED_USERNAME` want one name, so the holder's name is outdated: the holder was renamed at the provider and has not joined since. The current owner gets the name, and the holder a name made with `rename-holder`'s settings for its provider. On the holder's next join, the provider's new name for it goes through the rules as a `PROVIDER_RENAME`.
- Another player on this proxy still plays under the account's username, such as a holder renamed while online who has not left yet. The player plays this session under a name made with `rename-holder`'s settings for the player's provider.

## messages.yml

The messages apply to every scenario.

<Setting
  name="pipeline.identity.synchronizationFailed"
  takes="a list of lines"
  affects="the message shown when Identica cannot update the account's username"
>

</Setting>

<Setting
  name="pipeline.policy.taken"
  takes="a list of lines"
  affects="the message a player is refused with when the conflict rules refuse them"
>

`{username}` is the username the player wanted.

</Setting>

<Setting
  name="pipeline.policy.holderReplaced"
  takes="a list of lines"
  affects="the message an online holder is disconnected with under `holderOnline: KICK_HOLDER`"
>

`{username}` is the username the holder lost and `{renamed}` the one they play under from the next join on.

</Setting>

```yaml
pipeline:
  policy:
    taken:
      - "<red>{username} is taken, please join with another name.</red>"
    holderReplaced:
      - "<yellow>The owner of {username} has joined. Join again to play as {renamed}.</yellow>"
```
